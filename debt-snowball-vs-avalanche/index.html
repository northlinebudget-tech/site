Debt Snowball vs Debt Avalanche: Which Should You Use?

Both methods pay every minimum and put all your extra money toward one debt at a time. When that debt is gone, its payment rolls into the next one. They differ only in the order.

SnowballAvalanche
Pays firstSmallest balanceHighest interest rate
StrengthQuick wins keep you goingLeast total interest
Best ifYou need motivationYou'll stick with it either way

Example

Credit card $3,000 at 24%, car loan $9,500 at 7%, student loan $15,000 at 5%.

  • Snowball order: credit card, car loan, student loan (smallest balance first).
  • Avalanche order: credit card, car loan, student loan (highest rate first).

Here both give the same order, because the smallest debt also has the highest rate. They differ when a small debt has a low rate: then avalanche saves more interest and snowball gives a faster first win.

How to choose

If the interest difference is small, pick the one you'll stick with. Many people start with snowball for motivation and switch once the small debts are gone.

Quick answers

What is the difference between debt snowball and avalanche?
Snowball pays the smallest balance first; avalanche pays the highest interest rate first. Both keep every minimum paid and roll each finished payment into the next debt.
Which saves more money?
The avalanche method usually saves more interest. The snowball method gives faster early wins, which helps some people stay consistent.
