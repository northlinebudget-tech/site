Sinking Funds: How to Save for Costs You Know Are Coming

A sinking fund is money you set aside a little at a time for a cost you know is coming: Christmas, car repairs, the yearly car registration. When the bill arrives, the money is already there.

The formula

(Goal − already saved) ÷ paychecks left before the date = amount per paycheck

Example: $1,200 for Christmas by December 1, $300 saved, 4 biweekly paychecks left: (1,200 − 300) ÷ 4 = $225 per paycheck.

Sinking fund ideas

  • Christmas and gifts
  • Birthdays
  • Car repairs and maintenance
  • Car registration and insurance paid yearly
  • Annual subscriptions
  • Vet visits
  • Home repairs
  • Back to school
  • Summer trip
  • Doctor and dentist copays

Where to keep it

A separate savings account keeps the money out of everyday spending. One account is enough; track each fund's balance in a spreadsheet.

Quick answers

What is a sinking fund?
Money you save a little at a time for a known future cost, such as Christmas, car repairs or a yearly bill, so it's covered when it arrives.
How do I calculate a sinking fund?
Subtract what you've already saved from the goal, then divide by the number of paychecks left before the date.
